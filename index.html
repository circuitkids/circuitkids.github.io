Why this matters

Many South African learners meet technology late, in a language that is not their own, or not at all. Slow data, few devices and few trained teachers can leave a bright child on the wrong side of the digital divide.

A child's language, school or background should never decide whether they get to create with technology.

  • AccessLessons and kits that work offline and on slow data.
  • LanguageEvery language is a doorway into computational thinking. Stories and examples in the child's own language.
  • BelongingTownship-inspired resources and heritage stories that children see themselves in.

Not another coding service. A way for more children to start.

What creators learn

One Saturday at a time, one project at a time. Creators start with play and logic - no screens needed - then bring their own drawings, voices and stories to life with code, and grow into designing and building their own games and programs. We are not only teaching coding: art, story, music and typing grow alongside.

By the end of the year, a creator can:

- Break a problem into small steps and solve it.
- Create their own stories and games with code.
- Program a real robot and a micro:bit.
- Stand up and share what they made with confidence.

Learning Tools and Platforms

Parents should know exactly what is on their child's screen. These are all the apps and tools we use - nothing else. Our learning phones and tablets are locked to learning apps only. Every app is free, has no adverts, needs no account and collects no data.

Cooper the STEM Robot - a screen-free coding robot. Children program it with buttons on the robot itself. No app, no screen.

OctoStudio - a free app from the MIT team behind Scratch. Drawings, voices and stories brought to life with code. Works offline.

micro:bit and the micro:bit app - a small pocket computer. Children code it from the free official app and send their projects over Bluetooth - no cable needed.

Microsoft MakeCode - the free website used to code the micro:bit. Blocks first - no typing needed to start.

Scratch - the free block-coding classic from MIT, on our coding stations.

CreateAI - a free tool from the micro:bit team. Children train simple AI on their own movements. No accounts, no personal data.
